00 / Premise

The problem

A debate is more than a
bag of keywords.

Debates are defined by conflict. A useful explanation must preserve that conflict—not flatten it.

The proposed system is designed to model statements from opposing speakers as structured arguments. Instead of counting phrases or hiding the reasoning in a black box, its method makes attacks, supports, and derivations explicit.

01

See the field

Argument relation graph

Arguments become nodes. Directed edges show where one argument attacks another, while acceptance semantics distinguish defensible positions from contested ones.

02

Follow the exchange

Dialogical explanation

Select an argument to unfold a dispute tree: a proponent advances a claim, an opponent challenges it, and each side responds in turn.

03

Inspect the reasoning

Logical explanation

Open an argument to trace its premises to its conclusion through a natural-deduction proof, then read the same derivation in natural language.

Why NDSA?

Explainable by design.

Natural Deduction–based Structured Argumentation (NDSA) combines abstract argumentation with proof structure. It can reason over inconsistent information while keeping the path from premises to claim visible.
